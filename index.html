Train Fever — 20-Minute Rule & Tractive-Effort Route Calculator

Two independent checks for a route you are planning in Train Fever: whether the trip clears the game's 20-minute passenger rule, and whether your locomotive has enough pull for the steepest grade on the route. Enter your own numbers — the math runs in your browser, nothing is saved or sent anywhere.

20-Minute Rule checker

Enter the station-to-station distance, the vehicle's top speed, and the line's frequency (minutes between departures) from your own line manager.

Assumption this tool makes, not a game-documented number Average wait time is estimated as frequency ÷ 2 — the standard queueing approximation for a passenger arriving at a random time between departures. The game itself does not publish a wait-time formula; this is this tool's own approximation, applied on top of the sourced 20-minute threshold (see Sources below).

Tractive-effort / grade-climb estimator

Standard real-world locomotive physics, not a game-specific constant (see Sources). Enter your locomotive's tractive effort rating, your total train weight, and the steepest gradient on the route. No specific in-game locomotive's numbers are hardcoded here — read your own values off the game's purchase/vehicle screen and enter them below.

Sources & last verified date

All claims below fetched directly 2026-10-14. No "best locomotive" or "optimal route" verdict is given anywhere on this page — only the computed numbers.

  1. 20-minute rule, existence and definition — corroborated by two independently-fetched Steam Community discussion threads (tt-forums.net's own Train Fever threads returned an anti-bot challenge page on direct fetch and could not be read this session; see README for the exact attempt). First: Train Fever discussion #3800525308873794060 — "the 20 minute timers counts travel to stations, waiting, transit, unloading and then travel to the source of the demand. All that needs to be under 20 minutes or [the trip] breaks," with the same post stating "the same principle applies for passengers routes." Second: Transport Fever discussion #1471967615881291513 — "In Train Fever the 20 minute rule had no bearing on people and goods that were already in transit. The game estimates the time it takes to go places and if it estimates it to take longer than the allowed time it simply does not make the trip in the first place," and separately confirms the rule was removed (not merely patched) in the sequel, Transport Fever — which is why this tool is scoped to Train Fever only.
  2. Average-wait approximation (frequency ÷ 2): this tool's own choice, not sourced to the game — stated explicitly above and not treated as a corroborated game fact.
  3. Tractive-effort / grade-resistance formula: public-domain railway engineering, not a Train Fever constant, so no in-game vehicle numbers are hardcoded. Corroborated by two independent sources stating the same figure in different units: republiclocomotive.com/locomotive-power-calculations — "For every ton of train weight in a grade, 20 pounds of tractive effort is needed for each 1% of slope" — and a second independent source stating the metric equivalent, "20 pounds or 9 kilograms of tractive effort... for every ton of weight times every percent of grade." Both reduce to the same physics this page implements: required force = weight × g × (grade% ÷ 100), with g = 9.80665 m/s² (sin θ ≈ grade fraction for the small angles used as track gradients). This page states explicitly that it omits rolling, aerodynamic and curve resistance — a simplification, not an unsourced extra term.
